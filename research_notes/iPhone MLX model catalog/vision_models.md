# Compact MLX vision-language models for Beacon on iPhone (September 29, 2026)

## Which repositories are ungated, licensed, image-capable, and realistically usable on iPhone?

### Takeaway
The strongest missing general-purpose additions are `mlx-community/LFM2.5-VL-1.6B-4bit` (best balanced choice), `mlx-community/FastVLM-0.5B-bf16` (speed-focused small choice), and `mlx-community/Ministral-3-3B-Instruct-2512-4bit` (higher-quality, higher-memory tier). `mlx-community/GLM-OCR-4bit` is compelling only as a clearly labeled document/OCR specialist. All four are public and ungated, image-capable, and no larger than 2.780 GB in their current snapshots.

- Liquid AI describes LFM2.5-VL as a 1.2B language backbone plus a 400M SigLIP2 NaFlex vision encoder, with 32K context, native-resolution handling, tiled high-resolution input, OCR/document use, and support for English, Arabic, Chinese, French, German, Japanese, Korean, and Spanish. The card reports 82.5 DocVQA, 64.5 TextVQA, 76.0 ChartQA, and 63.9 OCRBench for FastVLM-0.5B. — [Apple FastVLM model card](https://huggingface.co/apple/FastVLM-0.5B)
- The Ministral conversion is explicitly generated for image prompting with `mlx-vlm`, is public/ungated, and carries Apache-2.0 metadata; its 2.745 GB weight file plus tokenizer/config files produce the 2.779 GB snapshot total. — [MLX conversion card](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/Ministral-3-3B-Instruct-2512-4bit); [HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/mlx-community/Ministral-3-3B-Instruct-2512-4bit?blobs=true)

- GLM-OCR is public, ungated, MIT-licensed, tagged for image-to-text/image-text-to-text, and its current repository consists of a 1,247,191,941-byte 4-bit weight file plus 6.9 MB of supporting files. — [HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/mlx-community/GLM-OCR-4bit?blobs=true)
- The official card calls it a 0.9B multimodal OCR model for document parsing and information extraction and reports 94.62 on OmniDocBench V1.5. It also warns that prompts are limited to text/formula/table recognition or strict-schema information extraction, which makes it too specialized to present as ordinary visual chat. — [GLM-OCR model card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-OCR)

### Inferences
- **Recommended catalog shape:** add LFM2.5 as the balanced mid-tier, FastVLM as a speed-oriented alternative near SmolVLM2's download size, and Ministral as a high-end option. Add GLM-OCR only if Beacon can label and route it as document extraction rather than promise general image Q&A. These roles are differentiated by the vendors' stated capabilities and by the 1.268/1.496/2.779 GB download steps. — [FastVLM card](https://huggingface.co/apple/FastVLM-0.5B); [LFM2.5 card](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-VL-1.6B); [Ministral API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/mlx-community/Ministral-3-3B-Instruct-2512-4bit?blobs=true); [GLM-OCR card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-OCR)
- Snapshot size is only the download/storage floor. Image tensors, KV cache, unquantized layers, and MLX working buffers increase peak memory, so the 2.779 GB Ministral model should be restricted to newer high-memory iPhones and tested on-device before catalog release. The repository itself contains 403,469,312 BF16 tensor elements in addition to quantized tensors. — [Ministral HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/mlx-community/Ministral-3-3B-Instruct-2512-4bit?blobs=true)
- FastVLM is worth including despite overlapping SmolVLM2 in storage because its explicit product advantage is latency/token efficiency, not model size alone. Its checkpoint is BF16, however, so it does not create a lower-storage tier than Beacon's approximately 1.02 GB SmolVLM2. — [FastVLM HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/mlx-community/FastVLM-0.5B-bf16?blobs=true); [Apple model card](https://huggingface.co/apple/FastVLM-0.5B)

### Gaps
- No primary source provides iPhone-specific peak-memory, thermal, tokens/second, or crash-rate measurements for these exact repositories under mlx-swift-lm 3.31.3. “Realistically usable” is therefore inferred from snapshot size, architecture scale, edge-oriented positioning, and explicit Swift support; physical-device validation remains necessary.
- HF's `safetensors.total` is an exact inventory of tensor elements in the repository, but quantized layouts and naming conventions mean it does not always equal the rounded parameter count in a model's product name. Both figures are reported where they differ. — [HF Hub safetensors metadata example](https://huggingface.co/api/models/mlx-community/LFM2.5-VL-1.6B-4bit?blobs=true)

## Does mlx-swift-lm 3.31.3 explicitly register each architecture and processor?

### Takeaway
Yes for all four recommendations, with two important implementation details: the FastVLM repository identifies as `llava_qwen2` rather than `fastvlm`, and Ministral/GLM use runtime selection behavior that must be read together with their processor files. The exact Beacon dependency is commit `1c05248...`, version 3.31.3.

### Cited Findings
- Beacon's resolved Swift package pins `mlx-swift-lm` **3.31.3** at commit `1c05248bb0899e2a7a4962b84d319cf12f4e12aa`. — [Beacon `Package.resolved`](../../beacon/beacon.xcodeproj/project.xcworkspace/xcshareddata/swiftpm/Package.resolved); [GitHub tag API](https://api.github.com/repos/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/git/refs/tags/3.31.3)
- Version 3.31.3's `VLMTypeRegistry` explicitly maps `fastvlm` and `llava_qwen2` to `FastVLM`, `lfm2_vl` and `lfm2-vl` to `LFM2VL`, `mistral3` to `Mistral3VLM`, and `glm_ocr` to `GlmOcr`. It also registers `qwen2_5_vl`, `qwen3_vl`, `paligemma`, `idefics3`, `smolvlm`, and `gemma3`. — [3.31.3 `VLMModelFactory.swift`](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.31.3/Libraries/MLXVLM/VLMModelFactory.swift)
- The same source explicitly registers `FastVLMProcessor`, `Lfm2VlProcessor`, `Mistral3Processor`, and `Glm46VProcessor`, as well as the Qwen, PaliGemma, Idefics3, SmolVLM, and Gemma processors. — [3.31.3 `VLMModelFactory.swift`](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.31.3/Libraries/MLXVLM/VLMModelFactory.swift)
- The FastVLM checkpoint says `model_type: llava_qwen2`, while both its processor and preferred preprocessor files identify `FastVLMProcessor`; every identifier has a matching 3.31.3 registry entry. — [FastVLM API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/mlx-community/FastVLM-0.5B-bf16?blobs=true); [preprocessor config](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/FastVLM-0.5B-bf16/blob/main/preprocessor_config.json)
- LFM2.5 uses `model_type: lfm2_vl` and `Lfm2VlProcessor`, both exact registry keys. The older LFM2 uses the hyphenated `lfm2-vl`, which is separately registered. — [LFM2.5 API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/mlx-community/LFM2.5-VL-1.6B-4bit?blobs=true); [LFM2.5 processor config](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/LFM2.5-VL-1.6B-4bit/blob/main/processor_config.json); [LFM2 API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/mlx-community/LFM2-VL-1.6B-4bit?blobs=true)
- Ministral's repository declares `PixtralProcessor`, but 3.31.3 explicitly overrides processor type to `Mistral3Processor` whenever `model_type == mistral3`; that override and processor are both in the factory source. — [Ministral processor config](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/Ministral-3-3B-Instruct-2512-4bit/blob/main/processor_config.json); [3.31.3 factory](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.31.3/Libraries/MLXVLM/VLMModelFactory.swift)
- GLM's `processor_config.json` says `GlmOcrProcessor`, which is not the registered key, but its `preprocessor_config.json` says `Glm46VProcessor`. The 3.31.3 loader explicitly prefers `preprocessor_config.json` when present, so the registered processor is selected. — [GLM processor config](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/GLM-OCR-4bit/blob/main/processor_config.json); [GLM preprocessor config](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/GLM-OCR-4bit/blob/main/preprocessor_config.json); [3.31.3 loader](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.31.3/Libraries/MLXVLM/VLMModelFactory.swift)
- The built-in `VLMRegistry` names FastVLM, Qwen2.5-VL, PaliGemma, SmolVLM, LFM2, LFM2.5, and Ministral constants, but its `all()` list omits LFM2/LFM2.5, Ministral, and GLM-OCR. This does not remove type/processor support: the factory can resolve an arbitrary repository ID and create it from `config.json`; it does mean these IDs are not all advertised by `VLMRegistry.all()`. — [3.31.3 factory and registry](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.31.3/Libraries/MLXVLM/VLMModelFactory.swift)

### Inferences
- Architecture and processor registration are a strong compatibility signal, but not proof of an end-to-end iPhone run. Beacon should smoke-test load, one-image preprocessing, first-token generation, and a follow-up turn for each exact repository, especially the GLM dual-config and Ministral override paths. — [3.31.3 factory](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.31.3/Libraries/MLXVLM/VLMModelFactory.swift)
- LFM2.5's omission from `VLMRegistry.all()` should not block a direct `ModelConfiguration(id:)` catalog entry, because its exact architecture and processor are registered; it should nevertheless be covered by an integration test to prevent confusion with “tried” built-ins. — [3.31.3 factory](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.31.3/Libraries/MLXVLM/VLMModelFactory.swift)

### Gaps
- The 3.31.3 README's “models tried” list lags its source registry and does not list FastVLM, LFM2.x, Ministral, or GLM-OCR, so the source is the authoritative support evidence but does not provide per-repository test results. — [3.31.3 MLXVLM README](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.31.3/Libraries/MLXVLM/README.md); [3.31.3 factory](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.31.3/Libraries/MLXVLM/VLMModelFactory.swift)

## Which named candidates should be included or excluded, and why?

### Takeaway
Include FastVLM 0.5B and LFM2.5-VL 1.6B; prefer LFM2.5 over original LFM2. Include Ministral 3B as the under-3-GB premium tier and GLM-OCR only as a specialist. Exclude Qwen2.5-VL 3B, PaliGemma, Qwen3-VL 4B, and old SmolVLM 2.2B from the immediate catalog because they fail the preferred download threshold, add restrictive licensing, duplicate existing choices, or offer a weaker product distinction.

### Inferences
- For a compact catalog rather than an architecture showcase, **LFM2.5 + FastVLM + Ministral** gives clearer user-facing choices than adding every compatible family: balanced, fast, and premium. GLM-OCR can be a fourth choice only when specialization is visible in the UI. — [LFM2.5 card](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-VL-1.6B); [FastVLM card](https://huggingface.co/apple/FastVLM-0.5B); [Ministral API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/mlx-community/Ministral-3-3B-Instruct-2512-4bit?blobs=true); [GLM-OCR card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-OCR)
- Qwen2.5-VL remains technically viable for an opt-in “large” section because it is only 89.7 MB over the decimal 3 GB preference and is explicitly named by the runtime. The stronger immediate reasons to defer it are peak-memory uncertainty and Qwen Research licensing, not lack of MLX Swift support. — [Qwen2.5 API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/mlx-community/Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct-4bit?blobs=true); [3.31.3 factory](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.31.3/Libraries/MLXVLM/VLMModelFactory.swift)
- PaliGemma is the weakest catalog fit among the named candidates: it is larger than both the stated preference and Ministral, uses an older fixed-resolution checkpoint, and carries a less straightforward license/access story. — [PaliGemma API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/mlx-community/paligemma-3b-mix-448-8bit?blobs=true); [processor config](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/paligemma-3b-mix-448-8bit/blob/main/preprocessor_config.json)

### Gaps
- The Apple AMLR and Liquid LFM 1.0 licenses are not SPDX-standard permissive licenses like Apache-2.0 or MIT. Legal review should confirm that Beacon's app-mediated downloads and intended commercial distribution comply before release. — [FastVLM license metadata](https://huggingface.co/apple/FastVLM-0.5B); [LFM2.5 license](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-VL-1.6B/blob/main/LICENSE)
- There is no controlled, same-runtime iPhone benchmark comparing these exact quantized repositories with Beacon's SmolVLM2 500M and Qwen2-VL 2B. The recommendation uses model-card benchmarks only to establish model intent and relative vendor-reported quality, not to claim verified Beacon performance.
